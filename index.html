The Impact of AI Detection Tools
on BNBU Students' Writing Anxiety
and Coping Strategies

EAP II Research Presentation

Introduction

The Problem with AI Detection

What are AIDTs?

Software (e.g., Turnitin) used by universities to identify AI-generated content in student work

The Bias

AIDTs produce high false-positive rates against non-native English speakers

(Liang et al., 2023)

Literature Review

Liang et al. (2023)

AIDTs mis-flag non-native English writing due to low linguistic perplexity

Cheng (2004)

SLWAI: measures writing anxiety across somatic, cognitive, and avoidance dimensions

Lazarus & Folkman (1984)

Stress-coping model: externalized vs. internalized strategies

Perkins et al. (2024)

Humanizer tools can effectively reduce AI detection scores

Balalle & Pannilage (2025)

AIDTs may damage trust between students and universities

Gap: Psychological impact on L2 writers in Chinese universities remains unexplored

Research Questions
  • RQ1 — To what extent do BNBU students experience or fear false-positive misjudgments from AIDTs?
  • RQ2 — What levels of academic writing anxiety do BNBU students experience in relation to AIDTs?
  • RQ3 — What strategies do BNBU students use to cope with AI detection in their academic writing?
Hypotheses
  • H1 — Most BNBU students experience or fear future false positives.
  • H2 — Students who have experienced false positives will express higher writing anxiety.
  • H3 — Students tend to employ multiple types of coping strategies in response to AIDTs.

Discussion

The Paradox

AI detection tools are designed to protect writing quality.

But the data shows students are
deliberately degrading their own writing
to avoid being flagged.

The tool designed to protect writing...
is damaging writing.

(Balalle & Pannilage, 2025)

Conclusion

RQ1

False-positive misjudgment was widely experienced or feared by BNBU students

RQ2

High writing anxiety, with avoidance behavior as the strongest dimension

RQ3

Multiple coping strategies used, including both tools and writing habit changes

Limitation: N=39, 94.9% freshmen, quantitative only
